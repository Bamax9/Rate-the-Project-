Window Projection

A Nebula Capsule 3 projector, a projection sheet and a window. Together they make a large display that can be seen from inside the house and from outside. Here is how the setup works.

ABC

1. The projector

The Nebula Capsule 3 is a compact, portable projector. It sits on a shelf in my room, a few feet back from the window.

2. The beam

The projector sends the image out through its lens as a cone of light that widens with distance. The farther back it sits, the larger the image.

3. The projection sheet

A projection sheet hangs over the window. Clear glass lets most of the light pass through, but the sheet scatters it, so the image shows up on its surface.

4. The mirrored image

From the other side of the sheet, the image appears reversed. Text flips, so the projector's mirror setting can correct it for whichever side you want to read from.

Inside view: reads the right way.

The projector sits on one side of the sheet, so viewers on the other side see the image from behind, which reverses left and right.

Setup

  1. Hang the projection sheet over the window.
  2. Dim the room. A darker space makes the image look brighter and sharper.
  3. Place the projector on a stable surface facing the window, a few feet back.
  4. Adjust the distance until the image fills the sheet.
  5. Set the focus and use keystone correction to square up the image.
  6. Use the projection mode setting to flip the image if text needs to read correctly from outside.
  7. Start playback.
Check with a parent before attaching anything to a window.

The projection sheet

Why it works: the sheet scatters the light, so the image shows on its surface and can be seen from both sides.

Without it: on plain clear glass, most of the light passes through and the image looks faint.

Lighting: daylight washes out the image because sunlight is far brighter than a small projector. Evening gives the best results.

Test different distances from the sheet to find the best balance between image size and brightness.

Safety

  • Never look directly into the lens.
  • Keep the vents clear so the projector does not overheat.
  • Place it where it will not be bumped or knocked over.
  • Get a parent's permission before hanging anything on a window.
A cool, stable projector lasts longer.
